# Hypoxia-Recombination-2026
Repository for R scripts to analyze the effects of hypoxia on meiotic recombination

## Citation

Rivera-Rincón N\*, Koury SA\*, Ghasemi Shiran M, Stevison LS. 2026. Meiotic recombination rate in *Drosophila melanogaster* is robust to hypoxic conditions. Unpublished manuscript.

\*These authors contributed equally to this work.

> **Note:** This is an unpublished manuscript. Citation details will be updated once a preprint is available.

## Abstract

Meiotic recombination is often described as a plastic trait that responds to environmental stress, but the evidence across stressors is mixed and the role of oxygen availability in particular remains poorly resolved. We tested whether chronic, mild hypoxia alters the rate of meiotic recombination on the X chromosome of *Drosophila melanogaster*. F<sub>1</sub> females were reared from oviposition through eclosion under one of three conditions: an outside-chamber normoxic control (*P*<sub>O<sub>2</sub></sub> ≈ 21 kPa), an in-chamber normoxic control (*P*<sub>O<sub>2</sub></sub> ≈ 21 kPa), and a hypoxic treatment (*P*<sub>O<sub>2</sub></sub> ≈ 8 kPa). To separate genuine meiotic effects from the confounding influence of differential survival, we paired a six-point multiply-marked test cross with marker-free crosses on identical outcrossed backgrounds, directly measured F<sub>1</sub> fecundity and F<sub>2</sub> egg-to-adult viability, and fit the resulting phenotypic class counts to an extended *Cx(Co)<sup>m</sup>* model that accounts for asymmetric meiosis and treatment, marker, and sex-specific mortality. Ovary RNA-seq from unmated F<sub>1</sub> females showed a transcriptional response consistent with hypoxic stress, including down-regulation of oxidative phosphorylation and ribosome pathways, up-regulation of stress and DNA damage response genes, and modest up-regulation of HIF pathway components. Hypoxia also reduced F<sub>1</sub> fecundity relative to in-chamber normoxia. Despite these clear physiological and transcriptional signatures of stress, we detected no effect of oxygen treatment on total X chromosome genetic length or on the strength of crossover interference, and no consistent effect on any of the five intervals examined. Recombination on the X chromosome of outbred *D. melanogaster* therefore appears robust to mild chronic hypoxia, even when the same exposure is sufficient to reshape ovarian gene expression and depress reproductive output. We suggest that hypoxia-induced plasticity in tracheal growth may buffer ovarian tissue from cellular oxygen limitation, and that the framework used here, combining marker-free viability controls with a process based meiotic model, offers a cleaner way to ask whether other stressors or genotypes produce real recombination plasticity rather than mortality artifacts.

## Repository structure

The repository has three directories: `code` (R scripts), `inputs` (raw data and analysis input files) and `outputs` (tables, figures and intermediate datasets).
